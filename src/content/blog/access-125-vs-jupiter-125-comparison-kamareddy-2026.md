## Suzuki Access 125 vs TVS Jupiter 125: Engine and Performance

Both scooters are powered by 125cc engines, but they have distinct characters. The Suzuki Access 125 uses a 124cc, 4-stroke, air-cooled engine that's known for its smoothness and refined power delivery. It feels peppy enough for city commutes and can handle the occasional highway stretch without much fuss. Many riders appreciate its linear acceleration.

On the other hand, the TVS Jupiter 125 features a 124.8cc, 4-stroke, air-cooled engine. While also smooth, it often feels a bit more responsive from a standstill, which can be a boon in stop-and-go traffic around Kamareddy. TVS has also focused on making this engine fuel-efficient, an important consideration for our local buyers.

### Power and Torque Output

Comparing the raw numbers, the Access 125 typically produces around 8.7 PS of power and 10 Nm of torque. The Jupiter 125 is very similar, often generating about 8.15 PS and 10.5 Nm of torque. In real-world riding, these differences are marginal, and both scooters offer sufficient power for their intended use as urban and semi-urban commuters.

## Access 125 vs Jupiter 125: Features and Technology

This is where the TVS Jupiter 125 often pulls ahead, especially in its higher variants. TVS has packed it with practical features that resonate with Indian families.

### Convenience Features

*   **External Fuel Filler:** The Jupiter 125 boasts an external fuel filler cap located on the front apron, which means you don't have to open the seat every time you refuel. This is a huge convenience, particularly when you have luggage or a pillion rider. The Access 125 still requires you to lift the seat.
*   **Under-seat Storage:** The Jupiter 125 offers a massive 33 litres of under-seat storage, which is among the best in its class. You can easily fit two helmets or a week's worth of groceries. The Access 125 provides around 21.8 litres, which is decent but not as generous.
*   **Digital Instrument Cluster:** Both scooters offer digital-analog or fully digital instrument clusters in their higher variants, providing information like trip meters, fuel gauges, and sometimes even real-time mileage indicators.

### Braking and Suspension

Both the Access 125 and Jupiter 125 come with telescopic front suspension and a mono-shock at the rear, offering a comfortable ride over varied road conditions, common in areas like Domakonda. Braking is handled by a disc brake at the front (in higher variants) and a drum brake at the rear, with a combined braking system (CBS) for added safety. This ensures good stopping power for everyday riding.

## Ride Comfort and Ergonomics

Both scooters offer a comfortable riding position, suitable for diverse Indian physiques. The Access 125 has a slightly lower seat height, which can be advantageous for shorter riders or for navigating crowded markets. The Jupiter 125, with its slightly longer seat, offers ample space for both rider and pillion, making it a comfortable option for families.

When it comes to suspension, both handle bumps and rough patches reasonably well. The Jupiter 125's larger front wheel (12-inch) compared to the Access 125's 10-inch rear wheel can contribute to a slightly better ride quality over uneven surfaces, which we frequently encounter on roads leading to Banswada.
